Living room

A full-width panelled wall with a backlit TV panel

The living room wall is panelled from end to end in a dark oak-effect finish, with a shadow gap of light along the ceiling. In the middle, a stone-effect panel sits behind the TV and is lit from behind, so the screen appears to float in a soft halo.

Below it, a long low unit runs almost the full width of the wall. The fronts are handleless and open with a push, the top matches the stone-effect panel above, and an open section in the middle keeps the soundbar and boxes within reach of a remote.

Second room

Slatted panels and a built-in fire

The second room takes the same idea in a lighter direction. Slim slatted panels are set against marble-effect panels, and a wall-hung unit carries a built-in electric fire with the TV and soundbar above.

Hanging the unit off the wall keeps the floor clear, which makes the room feel bigger and is easier to clean under. A slim brass-effect trim under the top ties in with the warm tones of the slats.

The details

What makes it work

Small decisions at the design stage are what make a media wall look built-in, not added on.

Lighting planned in

The halo behind the TV panel and the wash of light along the ceiling are designed in from the start, so there are no visible fittings or trailing cables.

No handles

Push-to-open drawers keep the fronts completely clean, so nothing interrupts the long line of the unit.

Made to the wall

Each wall is measured and the panels are cut to suit it, so the joints line up and the panelling finishes neatly at doors and corners.

Room for the kit

Open sections for the soundbar and boxes, with cable routes behind the panels, keep the technology tidy.
